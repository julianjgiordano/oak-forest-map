Who owns Greene County?

Coal companies own about one in seven acres of Greene County, Pennsylvania: 51,830 acres, or 14.1 percent of its land.

About this map

This map shows who owns the surface of every parcel in Greene County, Pennsylvania, with land owned by coal companies in color. It began with the village of Oak Forest, where coal companies own 26 of 31 parcels, and asks how far that pattern extends. Click a parcel to see its owner, its recorded sales and the mines beneath it.

Key findings

  • Coal companies own 51,830 acres of Greene County's surface, about one acre in seven (14.1 percent of the county's land area). Two groups hold most of it: CONSOL / Core and affiliates (29,019 acres) and Iron Senergy and its predecessors (16,278 acres). Each owns more of the county than the Pennsylvania Game Commission (16,145 acres).
  • Inside the permit areas of the five operating underground mines, coal companies own 23.9 percent of the land, against 8.8 percent in the rest of the county.
  • Where both dates are known, coal companies bought three in four parcels above longwall panels before the panel beneath was mined (median: two years before).
  • About 1,860 acres of game lands came straight from coal companies, mostly a 2018 donation by CONSOL, CNX and affiliates for an Indiana bat conservation area.

What counts as a coal company

An owner counts as a coal company if its principal business is producing coal, or if it is a land-holding affiliate of a coal producer, or if it holds land that was bought for or used in coal mining. Contractors, utilities and cleanup bodies are not counted. Owners are grouped as CONSOL / Core and affiliates (including CNX Land and Consolidation Coal), Iron Senergy and predecessors (the ownership chain of the Cumberland and Emerald mines, including Contura, Alpha, Foundation, RAG, Cyprus and U.S. Steel Mining and their land companies) and other coal companies (including Duquesne Light's Warwick Mine land). The map shows ownership of the surface only, not of the coal beneath it.

Limits

  • Sales histories are partial. Outside Oak Forest they come from the county's online records, which mostly begin in the 1980s. The seller shown for a sale is the previous buyer in the record, which is usually but not always the seller on the deed.
  • Prices are as recorded. The county often lists $0 where a deed states a real price, and $1 or $10 sums are usually mergers, transfers within a family or company, or nominal "consideration." A deed covering several parcels states one total price.
  • Assessment records show the owner of record for taxation, not legal title. Acreage in the legend is the county's listed acreage.
  • "Inside a mine permit area" is not the same as "above the mine": permits include land that is never mined beneath.

Names and privacy

Owners' names come from public deed and tax records and are shown as recorded. No street addresses are shown. People named on this map, or their families, can ask for a correction or for a name to be withheld.
