A block of land about 2.4 miles long and half a mile wide around the village of Oak Forest, Greene County, Pennsylvania: wooded ridges and open valleys, Pursley Creek, Oak Forest Road, a 138 kV power line and the Pursley substation, and the houses of the village. Beneath it, 600 to 800 feet down and following the gentle dip of the coal seam, a red slab marks longwall panel 75 of the Cumberland Mine, 11,221 feet long and 1,393 feet wide, mined from July 2022 to January 2023. It passes under the village. A dotted red line traces its outline on the ground above.

Sources and notes

Ground: USGS 3DEP lidar. Seam: PA DEP coal seam elevations (Cumberland Mine maps). Panels: PA DEP longwall mining panels. Streams traced from lidar. Trees: PEMA 2022 orthophoto; heights estimated. Buildings: Oak Forest traced from the 2022 orthophoto; elsewhere Microsoft building footprints. Roads, power lines: orthophoto, OpenStreetMap. No vertical exaggeration unless "3×" is chosen; the panel is drawn 10× its 7 ft height (seam height estimated); tree and building heights are estimates. Longwall face positions at month ends: Cumberland Mine subsidence map, sheet 31-4; between month ends the face is drawn moving at an even pace, and where a panel's first or last stretch has no date, its timing is estimated. Neighbouring panels: PA DEP longwall panels, each placed on its own fit to the seam elevations. Longwall in the "Shearer" close-up, at true size: a generic double-drum ranging shearer (spiral drums with picks, water sprays) and two-leg shields 1.75 m wide (about 240 across the face) with face sprags, control units and hoses, modelled on footage of the Cumberland longwall ("Real colours" shows the orange shearer and yellow shields seen there); behind the shearer each shield lowers, moves forward one web and resets, and the conveyor is then pushed over. Cut coal falls onto the face conveyor and is carried to the headgate, where it is crushed and loaded onto the stageloader and the belt. The headgate is taken to be the north side, beside unmined coal, and the tailgate the side next to the mined-out panel 74, as is usual. Miners, the headgate equipment, stoppings, a scoop and the tailgate's standing supports are illustrative. The section beside the shearer shows the coal and the rock above it, as an illustrative column for the Pittsburgh seam (claystone floor; coal with thin partings; draw slate and roof coal; shale, siltstone, sandstone, limestone): behind the shields the lower beds hang, break off and cave in, and the beds above bend down over the gob, opening gaps between them (published studies put the caved zone at 2 to 8 times the mining height). Rock falls, dust and roof breaks are illustrative. The shearer cuts a 42-inch web on each pass across the face, in both directions (Cumberland's practice as reported in American Longwall Magazine, 2005), so panel 75 took about 3,200 passes; its position along the face is worked out from the face advance, not recorded. A real pass took about 35 minutes. Gate roads: three entries on each side of the panel with their pillars and crosscuts, measured on sheet 31-4 and continued at the same spacing beyond the sheet; a sketch, not a survey. Pillars are drawn as tall as the panel. Subsidence is modelled, not measured: about 4 ft at the panel centre (55% of a 7 ft mining height), reaching about 200 ft beyond the edges (15° angle of draw), developing over about seven months after the face passes; drawn 10× deeper. Cracks above the panel show the broken rock over a longwall in a general way. More on sources and methods. Made with three.js. Keys: arrows turn the view, + and − zoom, R resets.
