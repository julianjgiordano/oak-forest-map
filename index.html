About this map

Oak Forest is one of the oldest villages in Greene County, Pennsylvania, settled long before the coal boom of the early twentieth century. Its land now lies within the orbit of the Cumberland Mine, one of the largest underground coal mines in the state. This map shows who owns each parcel in the village today and, for the village's 31 parcels, how each changed hands, deed by deed.

Key findings

  • Coal companies own 26 of the village's 31 parcels, covering 255 of its 474 listed acres (54 percent). The share is lower by acreage because several large farm tracts at the village edge remain privately owned.
  • Coal companies first bought village parcels in three waves: 1998–2003, 2007–2011 and 2016–2021. Nine parcels were first bought in 2017 alone.
  • Two parcels were bought by a coal company and later sold back to private owners. In one 1999 sale, the company kept the coal, oil and gas, the right to let the surface subside, a pipeline easement for coal-bed methane, and a right of entry until reclamation was complete.

What counts as a coal company

An owner counts as a coal company if its principal business is producing coal, or if it is a land-holding affiliate of such a company (for example a "land" subsidiary that holds surface property for a mine). Contractors, utilities and cleanup bodies are not counted. Here the coal owners are all in the ownership chain of the Cumberland and Emerald mines, grouped as Iron Senergy and predecessors. County records still list most parcels under Contura Pennsylvania Land LLC (spelled several ways), a predecessor company in that chain; the map groups the spellings and shows each name as recorded. Earlier coal owners include Pennsylvania Land Holdings. The map shows ownership of the surface only, not of the coal beneath it.

Sources

  • Deeds for the 31 village parcels, read at the Greene County Recorder of Deeds, Waynesburg, and transcribed by Julian Giordano (137 deed entries, 1898–2022). Each entry gives the deed book and page. Where a deed or the county shows $0 or a nominal sum, the price was calculated from the realty transfer tax recorded on the deed.
  • Ownership, acreage and coal classification: Greene County assessment records via Vision Government Solutions, accessed 17–18 November 2026, classified in the Greene County Coal-Company Land Ownership Dataset, version 5.6.
  • Parcel boundaries: Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection, PA_Parcels map service. Boundaries are approximate and do not always follow current parcel lines.
  • Base maps: OpenStreetMap contributors via OpenFreeMap; elevation from Mapzen/AWS Terrain Tiles (USGS 3DEP); satellite imagery © Mapbox, © Maxar.

Limits

  • Chains of title are partial. Most could be traced further back; none yet reaches the founding of the village.
  • Prices are as recorded. $0, $1 and $10 sums are usually mergers, transfers within a family, or nominal "consideration," not sale prices; they are marked "nominal." A deed covering several parcels states one total price for all of them.
  • Parcels outside the village show county assessment data only; their deeds have not been researched.
  • Assessment records show the owner of record for taxation, not legal title.

Names and privacy

Owners' names come from public deed and tax records. No street addresses are shown. People named on this map, or their families, can ask for a correction or for a name to be withheld.

Terms

Deed book/page
Where a deed is recorded at the county Recorder of Deeds; "0504/3047" is Deed Book 504, page 3047.
Consideration
The price stated in a deed.
et ux
"and wife" (Latin et uxor): the property was conveyed to a married couple.
et con
"and husband."
et al
"and others."
Surface / coal rights
In Greene County the coal was usually sold separately from the land above it, often generations ago. This map concerns the surface.
