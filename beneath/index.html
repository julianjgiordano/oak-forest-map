A block of land about 2.4 miles long and half a mile wide around the village of Oak Forest, Greene County, Pennsylvania: wooded ridges and open valleys, Pursley Creek, Oak Forest Road, a 138 kV power line and the Pursley substation, and the houses of the village. Beneath it, 600 to 800 feet down and following the gentle dip of the coal seam, a red slab marks longwall panel 75 of the Cumberland Mine, 11,221 feet long and 1,393 feet wide, mined from July 2022 to January 2023. It passes under the village. A dotted red line traces its outline on the ground above.

Sources and notes

Ground: USGS 3DEP lidar. Seam: PA DEP coal seam elevations (Cumberland Mine maps). Panels: PA DEP longwall mining panels. Streams traced from lidar. Trees: PEMA 2022 orthophoto; heights estimated. Buildings: Oak Forest traced from the 2022 orthophoto; elsewhere Microsoft building footprints. Roads, power lines: orthophoto, OpenStreetMap. No vertical exaggeration unless "3×" is chosen; the panel is drawn 10× its 7 ft height (seam height estimated); tree and building heights are estimates. Longwall face positions at month ends: Cumberland Mine subsidence map, sheet 31-4; between month ends the face is drawn moving at an even pace, and where a panel's first or last stretch has no date, its timing is estimated. Neighbouring panels: PA DEP longwall panels, each placed on its own fit to the seam elevations. Longwall shearer drawn to scale: it cuts a 42-inch web on each pass across the face, in both directions (Cumberland's practice as reported in American Longwall Magazine, 2005), so panel 75 took about 3,200 passes; its position along the face is worked out from the face advance, not recorded. Gate roads: three entries on each side of the panel with their pillars and crosscuts, measured on sheet 31-4 and continued at the same spacing beyond the sheet; a sketch, not a survey. Shearer and pillars are drawn 10× their height, like the panel. Made with three.js. Keys: arrows turn the view, + and − zoom, R resets.
